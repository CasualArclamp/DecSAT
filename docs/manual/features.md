# DecSAT manual: Features and test status

This page lists each function of DecSAT and its test status. [Back to the README](../../README.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| Waterfall + spectrum over the whole span, zoom and pan | ✅ |
| Carrier detection: every carrier marked with its symbol rate; slow ones (a 9.6 kS/s link in a 20 MS/s span) told from CW tones and spurs by a finer look | ✅ |
| VFOs: draw, drag, resize, click a carrier to claim it; one thread each | ✅ |
| **Identify** — blind symbol rate, roll-off, constellation, DVB-S2/S2X detection | ✅ |
| **Survey**: every clean carrier in the span tried in turn, a few at a time — Identify, then its decoder with a time to lock — with the outcome, the model, what it carries and its payload listed, and saved as CSV | ✅ |
| DVB-S2/S2X PL demodulation: frame lock, MODCOD per frame (ACM) | ✅ |
| Carrier recovery: a locked constellation, Es/N0 (the MER) and Eb/N0, for Identify and DVB-S2 VFOs | ✅ |
| **DVB-S → MPEG-TS** (EN 300 421): code rate, rotation and inversion found blind; Viterbi, RS, the same TS outputs | ✅ |
| **TPC 2964 → IP / TS / voice** (Intelsat IESS-315 turbo product code, BPSK/QPSK/8PSK) — and **Comtech TPC 7/8** (16K block), worked out from a live carrier: frame structure, scrambling and payload found from the signal; HDLC → IP (Cisco HDLC, PPP, Frame Relay), Comtech EDMAC → bridged Ethernet → IP, MPEG-TS, MPEG audio frames (a programme feed: listen, record `.mp2`), E1, Comtech D&I++ voice (one timeslot or several) and 64 kbit/s timeslots in HDLC packets — a trunk's voice channels, its IP, and the ADS-B and radar in it | ✅ confirmed on live Comtech CDM-600L and CDM-570L carriers |
| **Q-Flex FastLink → data and voice** (Paradise, QPSK 0.710): sync word, the (2880, 2048) LDPC code and the frame scrambler, all measured from a live carrier; inside, Paradise's closed-network + ESC framing and a 257-bit TDM multiplex (sixteen 8 kbit/s channels, metered live so speech shows); a call's G.728 voice put together from its four channels — listen, record `.wav`; the 2–4 × faster links' multiplex, its HDLC timeslot (bridged Ethernet → IP) and its G.728 calls | ✅ decodes live Q-Flex links down to the calls' speech, the faster ones' IP too |
| **Viterbi → data**: the K = 7 code (IESS-308/309 SCPC, DVB-S) at rate 1/2–7/8, puncturing and orientation found blind and followed when the carrier turns — and **any other rate-½ convolutional code up to K = 10** on BPSK or QPSK, its generators read off the parity check its stream satisfies (K = 9 (561, 753), K = 5, CCSDS-style inverted outputs …); then the payload search — with or without differential decoding — for HDLC, TS, E1, D&I++, Paradise or IBS/SMS (IESS-309, 16/15) framing, or just the scrambler from the idle fill | ✅ decodes a live 10.24 kBd IESS-308 carrier to its IBS frames (rate 1/2, differential, V.35) |
| **Carrier ID (DVB-CID, ETSI TS 103 129)**: the spread-spectrum identifier under a carrier — the uplink modulator's unique ID (and MAC), position, telephone and text | ✅ to the specification, on synthetic carriers (no CID among the recordings yet) |
| **Comtech CDM modems** (one decoder entry): the type found by itself — TPC 2964, K = 7 Viterbi or FastLink from the first look, the model from the data — or chosen: CDM-600L voice (TPC → D&I++ → G.728), CDM-570L TPC (→ IP / TS), CDM-570L Viterbi, CDM-570L trunk (voice + IP with ADS-B and radar); the G.728 voice in every channel played as soon as speech is found — no ▶, so several carriers can be heard at once, one voice at a time if you like | ✅ on live CDM-600L and CDM-570L carriers |
| **IBS programme audio → apt-X**: IBS at n × 64 kbit/s (IESS-309: G.704-shaped frames under the IBS synchronous scrambler, x¹⁵+x¹⁴+1 restarted every 128 frames — found from the signal) carrying a studio link's Standard apt-X (apt-X 100): codeword alignment, stereo and its AutoSync word found blind, listen, record `.wav`. The 0–4 kHz band decodes exactly; 4–16 kHz uses step-size tables fitted to the carrier (Standard apt-X's are not published) | 🟡 on a live 256 kbit/s Comtech IBS carrier: stereo 32 kHz, full band (upper bands approximate) |
| **Enhanced apt-X in a transport stream**: a broadcaster's contribution feed — stereo programmes as private streams (stream type 9Bh, private data indicator `APTx`, 576 kbit/s each). 24-bit Enhanced apt-X at 48 kHz, its framing and codeword packing worked out from the stream: it is aptX HD's decoder with the two last bits of the upper bands carried as parity. Named in the programme list and played by **Play in the app** (decoded by the app itself, no ffmpeg); `aptx_wav` writes each service to a `.wav` | 🟡 on a recorded Ku-band multiplex (six services, bit-exact against FFmpeg's aptX HD on repacked vectors); the in-app player not yet tried on a live carrier |
| **E1 voice**: G.704 E1 or Comtech Drop & Insert++ timeslots, G.711 A-law — levels per channel, listen, record `.wav`; G.728 LD-CELP 16 kbit/s voice in two bits of a timeslot — up to three such channels in one timeslot — found and decoded | ✅ (G.728 confirmed on live CDM-600L and CDM-570L voice) |
| **Radar feeds** on CDM-570/570L links: track reports in IBM bisync blocks, **ASTERIX** categories 034 and 048 in HDLC (the Mode S registers in them read too) and ADS-B (category 021) in IP — a track table, a CSV log, and the **radar scope**: a PPI with trails and leaders, a map, airports and runways, ADS-B aircraft over a radar's picture, callsigns from a decoded ADS-B feed or (switched on) adsb.lol | ✅ on live carriers |
| **Radar station monitoring** (an RCMS multicast in Frame Relay on a CDM-570L trunk): the station's units and the points they report, beside the scope | ✅ on a live trunk (what the points mean is open) |
| **Generic PSK/APSK/QAM → symbols** (`.bin`, one byte per symbol), BPSK…32APSK and 8/16/64QAM, for non-DVB carriers; on BPSK and QPSK it looks for a convolutional code meanwhile (as the Viterbi decoder does, given up after a while) and decodes what it finds through the payload search — or says there is none (*a block code or uncoded*) | ✅ |
| **TDMA bursts**: one carrier or a whole MF-TDMA group in a VFO — bursts found and demodulated one by one, the bursts' layout (preamble, pilots, fixed fields, idle fill) read blind, the slot grid, the frame and the terminals, a rate-½ convolutional code looked for in the bursts' data; a burst map; each burst's data symbols to a `.bursts` file. **SCPC or TDMA** per VFO (and for new VFOs): bursty bands found on the waterfall apart from the continuous carriers | ✅ analyser and demodulator on a live 3-carrier group; the coding inside is next |
| **IS-19 DAMA outbound → packets and call signalling**: the 102.4 kBd QPSK outbounds of a DAMA telephony network on Intelsat 19 (vendor unknown — frame, pilots, scrambler and packet stream all measured from a recording): frames found and read the right way up, carrier slips put right from the pilots, the packet stream descrambled, and the **request acks** and **channel assignments** picked out — an assignment goes out ~0.4 s before a call's two carriers key up; every packet to a `.csv` on Record | ✅ on a live recording: every call set-up in it, to the millisecond |
| **Satellite beacons**: the carrier tracked and measured — frequency (absolute, with the LO), drift, C/N0, level, charted — and any telemetry on it found blind: a data subcarrier phase-modulating the carrier (PCM/PSK/PM), data straight on its phase (PCM/PM) or a suppressed carrier (BPSK/QPSK); NRZ or bi-phase; minor frames on IRIG 106's sync words, CCSDS's marker or one found blind, in any polarity or differential coding; the minor-frame counter and the major frame as a map of which words change, any word plotted against time; CCSDS Reed–Solomon (dual basis, interleaved), randomiser, the K = 7 code and TM/AOS transfer frames; a pointing tone that rises with the level; frames and the carrier's log to files | ✅ on live beacons — Intelsat 19's: 4800 bit/s bi-phase on a 48 kHz subcarrier, IRIG frames; Eutelsat 172B's: 9600 symbol/s NRZ on a 76.8 kHz subcarrier, K = 7 coded CCSDS TM frames — every frame |
| **Inmarsat Classic Aero → ACARS, ADS-C, voice**: the L-band aeronautical service on a VFO, the channel named from the signal: the P channel (ground to aircraft, 600, 1200 or 10500 bit/s), R and T channel bursts (aircraft to ground) and C channel telephone calls (8400 bit/s AMBE, 21000 bit/s 9.6 kbit/s MP-LPC). Signalling in words, ACARS messages with ADS-C positions read, voice played. [DecAero](https://github.com/CasualArclamp/DecAero)'s receiver, built in. | ✅ on recordings: P channels at 600, 1200 and 10500 bit/s with ACARS, and a 21000 bit/s call. Bursts and 8400 bit/s voice work in DecAero and are not yet tried here |
| **Inmarsat GMR-2+ (IsatPhone) → sync, System Information**: the 50 kHz forward control carriers of Inmarsat's satellite phone network on a VFO: the sync channel (spot-beam colour codes, frame number) and the broadcast System Information (network, location area, neighbour beams, timing, the beam map). Its coding was worked out from recordings: it is not in the GMR-2 specification. | ✅ on a recording of channel 88: every sync and broadcast block but a few decodes |
| **QO-100's PSK beacon** (AO-40's uncoded format, 400 bit/s): frames with CRC, the blocks as text, housekeeping values when sent; also from a sound card in the small `qo100-beacon` app | ✅ on WebSDR recordings of 10489.750 MHz, every frame's CRC good, and the FEC block after each decoded |
| Narrow carriers: VFOs down to 500 Hz, ~10 kBd SCPC carriers lock; short bursts landing on a carrier (Bluetooth leaking into the HackRF) coasted through | ✅ |
| IQ recorder and spectrum-only VFOs | ✅ |
| IQ file replay (`cs8`, `cu8`, `cs16`, `cs24`, `cf32`; two-channel `.wav` from SDR#, HDSDR, SDRuno, SDR++ with format, rate and centre from the header), rate/centre from file names | ✅ |
| **Live HackRF One**, 2–20 MS/s, pure Rust over USB (no DLLs), LNB LO, an input-level warning when the gain is too high | ✅ |
| **Live Fobos SDR** (RigExpert), 8–50 MS/s, 14 bits: RigExpert's library loaded at run time (agile or stock firmware), the same waterfall, VFOs, retuning and decoders as the HackRF | ✅ used on a real board under Windows, DVB-S2 TS and IP carriers at 20 and 50 MS/s |
| **Live Airspy Mini / R2**, 3/6 MS/s (2.5/10 on an R2), 12 bits, 24–1750 MHz: pure Rust over USB (`libairspy-rs`, no DLL), bias tee on request | ✅ tested live: a Mini at 6 MS/s (QO-100), an R2 at 10 MS/s |
| **Live ADALM-PLUTO / Pluto+ / LibreSDR**, 0.26–61.44 MS/s, 70 MHz–6 GHz: pure Rust over USB or the network (no libiio, no DLL), 8-bit IQ on the Tezuka firmware, receive only | ⚠️ tested against a simulated Pluto only; not yet tried on hardware |
| **📷 Snapshots** of the spectrum and waterfall (or the window) to PNG, captioned; **session log** to CSV; **PCAP in parts** for tools that read finished captures; the **satellite named** from the orbital position a NIT or RCS map signals | ✅ |
| **Network IQ**: raw IQ from another program, so any radio it drives works (an SDRplay RSPduo under SDR++, say), on Windows or Linux. SDR++'s IQ Exporter, GNU Radio's TCP/UDP sinks, rtl_tcp (recognised and tuned); `tcp://` connects, `listen://` waits for a sender, `udp://` binds; int8, uint8, int16, int32, float32; the rate, the sender's frequency and the LNB LO entered (the stream carries none) | ✅ on simulated carriers over TCP and UDP |
| **LDPC + BCH → BBFRAMEs**: all 21 S2 and 31 S2X codes, BBHEADER, stream info, payload rate | ✅ |
| **S2X**: 8-bit PLS code, all 55 normal/short MODCODs — 2+4+2 8APSK to 256APSK, the new interleavers | ✅ |
| **S2X VL-SNR**: the VL-SNR header, pi/2-BPSK (and spread), medium FECFRAMEs, all 9 MODCODs — down to about 0 dB Es/N0 so far | ✅ |
| PL scrambling: the preferred sequences of S2X Table 19e found from the pilots | ✅ |
| **S2X baseband**: the low roll-offs (0.15/0.10/0.05, signalled by RO alternating with `11`), GSE-HEM (GSE sliced across BBFRAMEs), GSE-Lite; **multistream** carriers read stream by stream — each ISI to its own stage: GSE, GSE-HEM, a transport stream, or transport packets in a generic stream (a VSAT hub's signalling) | ✅ live on a Newtec hub's S2X ACM outbound (0.05 roll-off, signalling on ISI 0, IP on ISI 1); GSE-HEM on test signals |
| **S2X superframing** (Annex E): super-frames found by their start sequence, their format read, and formats 0 (S2X) and 1 (S2 legacy) decoded with the super-frame pilots on or off; the other formats named | ✅ on test signals (none seen live yet) |
| **ACM terminal table**: each GSE label (a terminal's MAC) — its bytes, its IP addresses and the MODCODs the hub sends it in, which is the terminal's link as the hub sees it | ✅ live: 25 terminals of a hub, QPSK 5/6 to 16APSK 5/6 |

| **GSE → IP → PCAP** + live IP stats, flows encrypted in transit marked as such; GSE variant detected from the data | ✅ |

## Signal galleries

A gallery of what each kind of carrier looks like on the waterfall, in the
constellation and against time, live and side by side: DVB-S2 with constant
and adaptive coding, DVB-S, NovelSat NS3/NS4, TPC 2964, Q-Flex FastLink,
K = 7 Viterbi links, TDMA and STDMA bursts, Comtech 8PSK, beacons and CW,
the HackRF's own artefacts, codes nothing decodes yet, DVB-CID and
transponders wider than the receiver. Each card says how to tell the
signal apart and what DecSAT does with it, with the numbers measured on
the carriers above.

[`docs/signal-gallery.html`](../signal-gallery.html) is one self-contained
page: download it and open it in a browser, or
[view it rendered](https://htmlpreview.github.io/?https://github.com/CasualArclamp/DecSAT/blob/main/docs/signal-gallery.html).
An Es/N0 slider sets the noise in every pane, Pause freezes them, and the
HackRF card's "Retune" button shows how spurs move with the tuning while
real carriers stay put.

[`docs/rcs-field-guide.html`](../rcs-field-guide.html) does the same for
satellite return links: DVB-RCS, the DVB-RCS2 burst kinds and the two
vendor returns measured on 172B, each with the pool waterfall, one
carrier's envelope, the burst's layout and its constellation, and a table
for telling them apart from the outbound
([view it rendered](https://htmlpreview.github.io/?https://github.com/CasualArclamp/DecSAT/blob/main/docs/rcs-field-guide.html)).

DVB-RCS Field Guide

The kinds of satellite return link a hub's terminals send on, each drawn the way DecSAT's Return pool shows it: the pool's waterfall, one carrier over two frames, how a burst is laid out, and its constellation. The standard ones follow EN 301 790 and EN 301 545-2. The two vendor returns are drawn from what DecSAT measured on Eutelsat 172B. Traffic and noise are simulated; the layouts and numbers are the spec's or the measurement's.

Which one is it? Look at the outbound first

A return link is hard to name from its bursts alone. The hub's forward link names it: open the outbound's VFO and see what signalling it carries.

How the panels are made: each case is a model of its return (carriers, frame, slot grid, burst lengths, guard, the layout of known symbols, the constellation), and the waterfall, envelope and constellation are computed from it with noise. Load and terminal behaviour are made up; frame lengths, slot counts, symbol rates and layouts are from the spec (EN 301 790 V1.5.1, EN 301 545-2 V1.3.1, Table A-1) or from DecSAT's 172B recordings (docs/STATUS.md).
